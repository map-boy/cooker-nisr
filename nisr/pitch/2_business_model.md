# BUSINESS MODEL (draft)

Principle: free for the young person; paid by the institutions that gain from better matching.

Users (free forever)
- Secondary and TVET students, unemployed youth, school leavers, people changing work.

Customers / payers (after the hackathon)
1. TVET schools, secondary schools, universities: guidance tool + cohort reports (subscription per school or per term).
2. NGOs, youth programmes, development partners: programme targeting + outcome tracking (project-based fee / grant-funded licence).
3. Districts / government agencies (WDA, MINEDUC, MIFOTRA, NISR): anonymised dashboard of talent supply vs. labour demand by district (service agreement).
4. Employers / training providers: sponsored "pathway" listings, clearly labelled, never mixed into the ranking.

Revenue streams
- School and programme licences; dashboard service contracts; grants; sponsored training listings (labelled).

Costs (low by design)
- Engine runs in the browser (near-zero per-user server cost); static hosting; small API + database for saved sessions.
- Main real cost: native-reviewed translations and keeping the demand data updated from NISR releases.

Why it can last
- Data-light PWA fits low-income phones and weak networks; Kinyarwanda first.
- Each completed session adds anonymised aggregate evidence (consent-based) that improves matching and feeds the dashboard.

Rules we keep
- Ranking is never for sale. Contacts only with consent; under-18 needs a guardian. No "pay first to get a job" paths.

IP note
